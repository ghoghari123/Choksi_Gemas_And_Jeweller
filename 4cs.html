THE FOUR CHARACTERISTICS

What Makes a Diamond Beautiful?

Every diamond has its own personality. The 4Cs provide a universal framework for describing four important characteristics of a diamond.

01 Cut 02 Color 03 Clarity 04 Carat
01
THE FIRST C

Cut

The Source of Brilliance

Cut refers to how well a diamond has been proportioned, polished and finished. It determines how effectively the stone interacts with light.

A well-cut diamond can return light through the top of the stone, creating brightness, fire and scintillation.

CUT GRADING
Excellent Exceptional
Very Good Very High
Good Good
Fair Moderate
Poor Limited

Brightness

The amount of white light reflected back through the top of the diamond.

Fire

The colorful flashes of light visible when a diamond interacts with light.

Scintillation

The flashes of light and dark created as a diamond or observer moves.

02
THE SECOND C

Color

The Subtle Art of Color

For most gem-quality diamonds, the color grading scale describes how close a diamond is to being colorless.

The less noticeable body color a diamond has, the higher it generally sits on the traditional D-to-Z scale.

COLOR SCALE
D Colorless
E Colorless
F Colorless
G Near Colorless
H Near Colorless
I Near Colorless
J Near Colorless
03
THE THIRD C

Clarity

The Story Within the Stone

Clarity describes the presence of internal characteristics called inclusions and external characteristics called blemishes.

Grading considers the number, size, position and visibility of these characteristics under standardized viewing conditions.

CLARITY SCALE
FL Flawless
IF Internally Flawless
VVS Very Very Slightly Included
VS Very Slightly Included
SI Slightly Included
I Included

Magnification

Clarity grades are determined under controlled magnification conditions.

Visibility

The visibility and position of characteristics influence grading.

Uniqueness

Internal characteristics can help make every natural diamond distinctive.

04
THE FOURTH C

Carat

Understanding Diamond Weight

Carat refers to the weight of a diamond. One carat is divided into 100 points, allowing diamond weight to be measured precisely.

Carat weight should not be confused with visual size. Two diamonds with the same carat weight can appear different depending on their shape and proportions.

4Cs QUESTIONS

Frequently Asked Questions

Which of the 4Cs is most important?

The importance of each C depends on your priorities. Cut is particularly important for how a diamond interacts with light, while color, clarity and carat contribute to its overall appearance.

Does higher carat always mean a better diamond?

No. Carat measures weight, not overall quality. A smaller diamond with an excellent cut can appear more brilliant than a larger diamond with weaker proportions.

What does a diamond's cut affect?

Cut affects how effectively the diamond reflects and returns light, influencing brightness, fire and scintillation.

Is a higher clarity grade always necessary?

Not necessarily. The ideal clarity grade depends on the diamond's characteristics, shape and how visible its inclusions are.

Should I choose color or clarity first?

There is no universal order. Your choice can depend on the diamond shape, metal, desired appearance and personal priorities.
